# Lore: writing a character people come back for

A character sheet locks the face. Lore locks the behaviour, and behaviour is what people follow.
Every AI influencer that has held an audience in 2026 has a bible whether or not the creator wrote it
down: Yang Mun has doctrine, Cringe Boy has a dad and Khabib, Mbok Geni has a pan that never leaves her
hands. Write yours before episode one or you will drift by episode five.

## The bible (one page, fill every line)
- One line: who they are + the one thing they always do.
- Visual lock: the paragraph you paste into every prompt. Three anchors a video model can hold across
  cuts (for Dennis: glasses, mustache, lanyard).
- Logo prop: the object that appears in every episode and never changes (pan, lanyard, clipboard).
- Backstory: 6-8 lines. Where they were, what broke, what they want now.
- Speech tics: 4-6 lines they say in every episode. These become the comments.
- Taboo: what the character never does. This is what keeps the account from becoming mean or unsafe.
- Off-screen cast: 2-3 people heard but not seen (the daughter filming, the wife in the kitchen).
- Nemesis: arrives around episode 10. Gives the season a shape.
- Season arc: the one goal the character is working toward across 10 episodes.

## The episode engine
One offender + one procedure + one cliffhanger. Every episode is the same shape and the audience learns
the shape by episode three. Numbered cases in the caption, PART number on the frame, outro card with the
handle. The cliffhanger is a caption line the audience can predict and argue about.

## Using Sherpa (optional)
Sherpa by Pocket FM (pocketfm.com/sherpa, in beta, free tier) is a fiction planner with a "Narrative
World Model": it tracks characters, relationships, locations and what each character knows, and it
suggests hooks and next-episode directions from what has already happened. Paste the bible in as the
premise and ask it for a 10-episode season with a cliffhanger per episode. Keep the episode engine rule
above; Sherpa will happily write you a three-act drama, and TikTok does not want one. It needs a Google,
Apple or email login; it is not required for anything in this plugin.

## Prompt discipline for the lore
- The character bible goes into Flow's "Character info" field once. Flow's agent reads it when you use
  the Agent mode; the manual mode does not, so the visual lock still has to be in every prompt.
- Dialogue in prompts is in quotes, short, one line per shot. Omni and Veo render native audio; one line
  per 8 seconds is the ceiling for lip-sync you can trust.
- The last line of the last shot is the cliffhanger. Never explain it.
